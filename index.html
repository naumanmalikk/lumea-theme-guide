1. Install the theme

1

Do not unzip lumea-theme.zip. Shopify needs the zip exactly as supplied.

2

In your Shopify admin, go to Online Store → Themes.

3

Under Theme library, click Add theme → Upload zip file, choose lumea-theme.zip, and click Upload file.

4

When it finishes, click Customize to open the theme editor. Everything from here on happens in that editor — you never need to touch code.

Publish the theme only once you're happy with it. Until you click Publish, your live store is untouched.

Working safely Customise Lumea while your old theme stays published. Shoppers see no change until you publish.

2. Set your colours and fonts

In the theme editor, open Theme settings (the gear icon at the bottom of the left sidebar).

Colours

Lumea uses five colour schemes. Every section lets you pick which one it uses, so changing a scheme updates everywhere it's applied at once.

SchemeUsed by default for
Scheme 1Page background — soft cream
Scheme 2White sections, hero text
Scheme 3Warm sand — benefits row, footer
Scheme 4Deep sage — announcement bar
Scheme 5Blush — newsletter

To rebrand the whole store quickly, change the Background, Text and Solid button background in Scheme 1, then work through the others.

Typography

Under Typography you can change the heading and body fonts, their sizes, letter spacing, and whether headings are uppercase. Defaults are Playfair Display for headings and Nunito Sans for body.

Using your own font

The font picker only offers Shopify's library. To use a face you have licensed, go to Online Store → Themes → Edit code → Assets → Add a new asset and upload the font files, then fill in Theme settings → Typography → Your own font.

  • Upload .woff2 (and optionally .woff for older browsers). Do not upload .ttf or .otf — they are several times larger for no visible gain.
  • Type the filenames exactly as they appear in Assets, e.g. apercu-regular.woff2.
  • Give the font any name you like — it is only used internally.
  • Choose whether it applies to headings, body text, or both.

Upload a bold file too if you have one; without it the browser will synthesise bold, which is usually acceptable but never as good.

Check your licence covers web embedding. Many desktop font licences do not.

3. Build your menus

Menus live in your Shopify admin, not the theme editor: Online Store → Navigation.

  • Main menu — appears in the header. Nested items become dropdowns automatically.
  • Footer menu — appears in the footer's Shop column.

Back in the theme editor, click the Header section to choose which menu it uses and to upload your logo.

Mega menu

Add a third level to any top-level item and it stops being a small dropdown and opens as a full-width panel instead, with each second-level item becoming a column heading. Two-level items keep the ordinary dropdown, so you do not have to restructure a menu you are happy with.

So a menu built like this:

  • Shop → Cleansers → Gentle Milk Cleanser, Balancing Gel Wash
  • Shop → Serums → Barrier Repair Serum, Brightening Serum

gives you a panel with Cleansers and Serums as headings and the products listed underneath. On a phone the same third level appears indented inside the slide-out menu.

To put a picture in the panel, add a Mega menu image block to the Header section and type the top-level item's name (for example Shop) into Show inside this menu item. Turn the whole behaviour off with Use a mega menu for three-level items.

4. Lay out the homepage

The Hero banner takes a Background video as well as an image. Set one and it plays muted and looped in place of the photo, with the image used as the poster frame while it loads. Keep the file short and under about 10MB — it competes with everything else for the first paint. Anyone who has asked their device to reduce motion sees the poster image instead, held still.

The homepage ships with nine sections already arranged. Click any of them in the left sidebar to edit, drag to reorder, or use the eye icon to hide one without deleting it.

Image sizes Hero: 2400 × 1400px. Product photos: square or 3:4, at least 1400px on the long edge. Collection images: 1200 × 1600px. Shopify handles resizing and serves the right size per device.

5. Set up product pages

Open any product from the page dropdown at the top of the theme editor, then click Product information.

The page is built from blocks you can reorder or remove: vendor, title, price, inventory status, description, buy buttons, three collapsible rows, and a share button.

Collapsible rows

Three are pre-filled — How to use, Ingredients, Shipping & returns. Edit the text, or add more with Add block → Collapsible row.

Variants

If a product has options (size, shade), they appear as selectable pills automatically. Price, availability and the image update as the shopper chooses — no setup needed.

Low stock

The Inventory status block shows "Only 3 left" when stock falls below a threshold you set. It only works if inventory tracking is on for that product.

6. Turn on collection filters

Lumea supports Shopify's storefront filters on both collection pages and search results — by availability, product type, price, colour swatches and more. The theme displays them; Shopify decides what they are. That means one setup step:

1

Install Search & Discovery from the Shopify App Store. It's free and made by Shopify.

2

Open the app and go to the Filters tab. Add the filters you want — Availability and Price are good defaults, then Product type or any tag you use.

3

Visit a collection page. The filter bar appears automatically. Nothing to switch on in the theme.

No filters showing? That's expected until the app is installed and at least one filter is configured. It isn't a theme fault — filters simply don't exist on your store yet. You can also hide the bar entirely in the theme editor under Product grid → Show filters.

Live search suggestions

Clicking the search icon opens a drawer that suggests products as the shopper types. It works out of the box with no setup. To send shoppers straight to a results page instead, switch off Theme settings → Search → Show live search suggestions.

7. The signature sections

Four sections are what set Lumea apart from a generic storefront. All four are optional — add or remove them from Customise › Sections like any other.

Skin quiz

A multi-step questionnaire that recommends products. Add one Question block per step, then Answer blocks inside it, each pointing at a product. The final step shows whatever the shopper's answers matched, with an add-to-cart button. Keep it to three questions — completion drops sharply after that.

Before & after

A draggable slider comparing two images. Both must be the same crop, same distance and same lighting, or the comparison looks fake the moment the handle moves. Set the starting handle position and the two labels in the section settings.

Please read. A before-and-after image is a claim about what your product does, and cosmetic efficacy claims are regulated in many countries. Use real, unedited photographs of the same person, and only claim what you can evidence. Do not present generated or retouched images as results.

Routine builder

Lets a shopper tick several products and add them in one go. Add a Step block per product — morning, evening, weekly, whatever suits your range. Steps whose product is sold out are disabled automatically, and unassigned steps stay hidden from shoppers while remaining visible to you in the editor.

Sticky add to cart

A slim bar that appears once the main add-to-cart button scrolls out of view. It mirrors the real button, so it disables itself when a sold-out variant is selected. Turn it on in the product template's Sticky add to cart block.

Countdown timer

Add the Countdown timer section anywhere and set Ends at in the format YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM using the 24-hour clock. The time is read in your shop's timezone, so a shopper in another country counts down to the same real moment rather than to midnight where they happen to be.

Choose what happens when it runs out: show a message, hide the whole section, or leave the digits at zero.

Other sections worth knowing

  • Blog posts — your latest articles anywhere, not just on the blog page.
  • Custom Liquid — app snippets or your own markup, without editing theme files.
  • Ingredients — an expandable list, useful for INCI or sourcing detail.
  • Video, Testimonials, FAQ — block-based, add as many rows as you need.

8. Popups and extras

All of these live in Theme settings, and every one of them except the back-to-top button ships switched off.

Email signup popup

Theme settings → Newsletter popup. Signups land in Customers tagged newsletter, exactly like the newsletter section. Set how many seconds to wait before it appears, and how many days to leave someone alone once they have closed or completed it. It never appears while you are working in the theme editor.

Back to top

Theme settings → Storefront extras. On by default. Appears once the shopper is past the distance you set, and moves keyboard focus to the top of the page as well as scrolling there.

Recent purchase popup and the viewing badge

Please read this before switching either on.

Shopify does not give a theme access to live order data or to how many people are on a page. There is no API for it. That means:

  • The recent purchase popup shows a real product, with its real price, image and link, taken from a collection you choose — but the shopper name and place come from the list you type into the settings, paired with the product at random.
  • The viewing badge generates its number in the browser from the range you set. It is not a measurement.

Presenting invented purchases or invented visitor numbers as though they were real is regulated in a number of markets, and buyers increasingly recognise these widgets for what they are. Both ship off. If you turn them on, use wording you would be comfortable defending, and check the rules where you sell.

Leaving the name list empty makes the popup show the product alone with no attribution, which is honest and still useful — it works as a "popular right now" prompt.

9. Motion, markets and search

Content fades in as it scrolls into view. Control it under Theme settings › Animations: turn it off entirely, choose fade / fade-up / fade-and-scale, and set the duration.

Animation switches off automatically for visitors whose device asks for reduced motion, and the theme is built so that if scripting fails the content is shown rather than hidden.

Country, currency and language

If you sell into more than one market through Shopify Markets, pickers appear in the footer automatically. A single-market store shows nothing, so there is no empty control to hide. Toggle it in the Footer section's settings.

Search engines

The theme outputs structured data (JSON-LD) for products, articles and collections — what lets Google show price, availability and review stars in results. Nothing to configure; it uses your existing product data.

10. Create your pages

  1. Go to Online Store → Pages → Add page in your admin.
  2. For a contact page, set the Theme template (bottom right) to contact. That adds a working contact form.
  3. Recommended pages: About, Contact, Shipping & Returns, Privacy Policy, Terms of Service.

12. Troubleshooting

The upload failed

Make sure you're uploading the zip without unzipping it first. If your browser auto-extracted it, re-download the file or re-compress the folder so assets, config, layout, sections, snippets and templates sit at the top level of the zip.

My homepage looks empty

The demo images aren't included — Shopify doesn't transfer images with a theme. Add your own in the Hero banner and Image with text sections. Products appear once you've assigned a collection to the Featured collection section.

The cart drawer isn't opening

Check Theme settings → Cart → Cart type is set to Slide-out drawer. If an app has replaced the cart, set it to Cart page instead.

Quick add says “Choose options” on some products

Quick add only appears on products with a single variant, because multi-variant products need a choice first. Shoppers click through to the product page instead.

Star ratings aren't showing

Ratings need a reviews app that writes to the standard reviews.rating metafield — Shopify's own Product Reviews app, Judge.me, Loox and Okendo all do. Then switch on Theme settings → Product cards → Show product rating.

I changed a setting and nothing happened

Some settings only affect certain sections. Check you're editing the right colour scheme — a section using Scheme 3 won't change when you edit Scheme 1.
